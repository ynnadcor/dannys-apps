The rules this lab shows

One stroke = 180° of crank. Four strokes = 720° = two crank revolutions. The cam turns once per cycle, so it runs at half crank speed.

Piston position: s(θ) = r(1−cosθ) + l − √(l² − r²sin²θ), where r = stroke/2 and l = rod length. The rod angle makes the piston move faster near TDC than near BDC. That asymmetry is the source of secondary vibration in the Balance Bay.

Real timing: intake opens before TDC and closes after BDC so the moving air column keeps filling the cylinder. When both valves are open near TDC, that is overlap.

textbookHeywood ch. 2 (slider-crank) and ch. 6 (valve timing); Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, ch. 2.

Heat & Efficiency

Why can't you just keep raising compression? The ideal Otto efficiency keeps rising with compression ratio, but real fuel knocks first.

Ideal Otto η
—
η = 1 − 1 / r^(γ−1)

textbookÇengel & Boles, Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach, Otto cycle section; Heywood §5.3. HyperPhysics: Otto cycle.

Why real engines fall short

Knock. Unburned end-gas self-ignites before the flame reaches it. The pressure spike hammers pistons and bearings. Higher compression, higher intake temperature and lower octane all make it worse. This sets the practical compression ceiling.

Heat loss. Heat escapes into walls and coolant during combustion and expansion. In the ideal cycle it does not.

Finite burn time and pumping. Combustion takes tens of crank degrees, and pulling air past a throttle costs work.

Lambda (λ). Actual air–fuel ratio ÷ stoichiometric ratio. For gasoline the stoichiometric ratio is about 14.7:1 by mass. λ = 1 lets a three-way catalyst clean the exhaust.

textbookHeywood ch. 9 (knock), ch. 12 (heat transfer), ch. 3 (stoichiometry).

The B58 and S68 run turbochargers with roughly 10.5–11:1 compression. Direct injection cools the charge, which helps them avoid knock at that ratio.

Firing Order & Balance

This is the heart of the inline-8 case. Pick a crankshaft layout, watch the eight pistons fire, and read whether the shaking forces and rocking couples cancel. Change one throw and see what breaks.

Crank throw angle per cylinder (cylinder 1 at the front)

What the numbers mean

Primary force repeats once per revolution: Σ cos(θ − φᵢ). Secondary force repeats twice per revolution and comes from the rod angle: Σ cos 2(θ − φᵢ), scaled by r/l. A couple weights each term by the cylinder's position along the crank, so even if forces cancel, the engine can still rock end to end.

The classic straight-8 crank is two inline-4 cranks set 90° apart and mirrored front to back (a “2-4-2” arrangement). Mirroring cancels the couples. Try the “two 4s, not mirrored” preset: forces still cancel, but a secondary rocking couple appears. That is why the mirrored layout won.

textbookTaylor, The Internal-Combustion Engine in Theory and Practice, Vol. 2, ch. 8 (balance); Heywood §2.2. firing order1-6-2-5-8-3-7-4 listed in Firing order (Wikipedia).

The inline-8's real weakness: crank twist

Perfect balance does not fix torsional vibration. Every firing pulse twists the crank slightly, and a long crank is a softer spring. Torsional stiffness of a shaft is k = GJ/L: longer means softer, so the twisting resonance drops into the rpm range you actually use.

Fixes used historically: a torsional damper on the nose, a stiffer crank with larger journal overlap, and taking power from the middle of the crank. Mercedes did this on the 1954–55 M196 Grand Prix straight-8, gearing drive off the crank's center.

BMW's B58 puts its timing chain at the transmission end for a related reason: the gearbox inertia there “significantly reduces the rotary oscillations” the chain sees (BMW ST1505, PDF p. 27). On a longer inline-8 crank, where you drive the cams from matters even more.

Lesson 29

Cooling Loop

An engine turns only part of its fuel energy into work. A large share goes into the coolant, and the cooling system has to carry it to the radiator. In a long inline-8, the hard part is getting the rear cylinders the same cold coolant as the front ones.

Thermostat

BMW primaryThe B58 has no conventional wax thermostat. An electrically driven heat management module uses a rotary valve to open and close coolant passages; the DME positions it from coolant temperature and a cylinder-head component-temperature sensor. The main coolant pump is belt-driven (the switchable pump in BMW's figure is not for the US). BMW ST1505 B58 Engine training manual, PDF pp. 42–44. exampleThe 88 °C opening point in this schematic is a generic wax-thermostat value used to teach the idea.

Heat-rejection calculator

Inline-8 problem: series flow vs. parallel feed

In series flow, each cylinder heats the coolant before it reaches the next one, so cylinder 8 runs hotter than cylinder 1. Hotter cylinders knock sooner and distort the bores and head differently. A distribution gallery (or cross-flow from the intake side to the exhaust side) evens it out. This is a real design decision your portfolio should argue for.

textbookHeywood ch. 12; Hoag & Dondlinger, Vehicular Engine Design, cooling chapter (coolant distribution and cross-flow heads). modelBars assume equal heat per cylinder and an 85 °C inlet.

From model to simulation

Sketch → Feature → Part → Assembly → Drawing. Every serious part starts as a constrained 2D sketch.

FEA (stress): split the part into small elements (the mesh), fix where it is held (boundary conditions), apply loads such as peak cylinder pressure × piston area, then read stress against the material's fatigue limit. Bad boundary conditions give confident, wrong answers.

CFD (flow): models air through ports and runners, or coolant through jackets. Same rule: the answer is only as good as the inlet and outlet conditions you set.

1-D engine simulation (GT-Power, Ricardo WAVE, or the free OpenWAM) predicts torque curves from geometry. Ask your school whether it has academic licenses.
