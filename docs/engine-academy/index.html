The rules this lab shows

One stroke = 180° of crank. Four strokes = 720° = two crank revolutions. The cam turns once per cycle, so it runs at half crank speed.

Piston position: s(θ) = r(1−cosθ) + l − √(l² − r²sin²θ), where r = stroke/2 and l = rod length. The rod angle makes the piston move faster near TDC than near BDC. That asymmetry is the source of secondary vibration in the Balance Bay.

Real timing: intake opens before TDC and closes after BDC so the moving air column keeps filling the cylinder. When both valves are open near TDC, that is overlap.

textbookHeywood ch. 2 (slider-crank) and ch. 6 (valve timing); Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, ch. 2.

Heat & Efficiency

Why can't you just keep raising compression? The ideal Otto efficiency keeps rising with compression ratio, but real fuel knocks first.

Ideal Otto η
—
η = 1 − 1 / r^(γ−1)

textbookÇengel & Boles, Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach, Otto cycle section; Heywood §5.3. HyperPhysics: Otto cycle.

Why real engines fall short

Knock. Unburned end-gas self-ignites before the flame reaches it. The pressure spike hammers pistons and bearings. Higher compression, higher intake temperature and lower octane all make it worse. This sets the practical compression ceiling.

Heat loss. Heat escapes into walls and coolant during combustion and expansion. In the ideal cycle it does not.

Finite burn time and pumping. Combustion takes tens of crank degrees, and pulling air past a throttle costs work.

Lambda (λ). Actual air–fuel ratio ÷ stoichiometric ratio. For ethanol-free gasoline the stoichiometric ratio is about 14.7:1 by mass; US E10 pump gasoline is about 14.0–14.1:1. λ = 1 lets a three-way catalyst clean the exhaust.

textbookHeywood ch. 9 (knock), ch. 12 (heat transfer), ch. 3 (stoichiometry).

The B58 and S68 run turbochargers with roughly 10.5–11:1 compression. Direct injection cools the charge, which helps them avoid knock at that ratio. Toyota's A25A runs 13:1 (14:1 in the hybrid version) with no turbo and an Atkinson cycle: the intake valve closes late, so the effective compression, counted from when the valve shuts, is lower than the 13:1 on the spec sheet. verifiedThe US EPA measured its effective ratio from about 7:1 (light and medium load) up to just over 11:1 (SAE 2019-01-0249). Firing Order & Balance

This is the heart of the inline-8 case. Pick a crankshaft layout, watch the eight pistons fire, and read whether the shaking forces and rocking couples cancel. Change one throw and see what breaks. The lab opens on an inline-6-style crank, which is wrong for eight cylinders, so you see what a broken crank looks like first.

What the numbers mean

Primary force repeats once per revolution: Σ cos(θ − φᵢ). Secondary force repeats twice per revolution and comes from the rod angle: Σ cos 2(θ − φᵢ), scaled by r/l. A couple weights each term by the cylinder's position along the crank, so even if forces cancel, the engine can still rock end to end.

The classic straight-8 crank is two inline-4 cranks set 90° apart and mirrored front to back (a “2-4-2” arrangement). Mirroring cancels the couples. Try the “two 4s, not mirrored” preset: forces still cancel, but a secondary rocking couple appears. That is why the mirrored layout won.

textbookTaylor, The Internal-Combustion Engine in Theory and Practice, Vol. 2, ch. 8 (balance); Heywood §2.2. firing order1-6-2-5-8-3-7-4 listed in Firing order (Wikipedia).

The inline-8's real weakness: crank twist

Perfect balance does not fix torsional vibration. Every firing pulse twists the crank slightly, and a long crank is a softer spring. The torsional stiffness of a round shaft is k = G·Ip/L. G is the shear modulus (about 80 GPa for steel), Ip = πd⁴/32 is the polar second moment of area of a solid shaft of diameter d, and L is the length. Longer means softer. The natural frequency is f = (1/2π)·√(k/J), where J is the mass moment of inertia (kg·m²) of what twists. A softer, heavier crank twists back and forth more slowly, so the resonance drops into the rpm range you actually use.

The baseline fix is a tuned torsional damper (viscous or rubber) on the free end of the crank, sized from a torsional calculation. BMW fits one even to the shorter B58 inline-6: item 6 in its belt-drive figure is the “crankshaft vibration damper” (BMW ST1505, PDF p. 34). Extra measures: a stiffer crank with larger journal overlap, and taking power from the middle of the crank. Mercedes did this on the 1954–55 M196 Grand Prix straight-8, taking the drive from the crank's center: its factory technical data list a “Hirth-type crankshaft with central power take-off”. The catch: a center gear and its bearings have to sit between cylinders 4 and 5, so the engine gets longer, and length is the inline-8's other big problem.

BMW's B58 puts its timing chain at the transmission end for a related reason: the gearbox inertia there “significantly reduces the rotary oscillations” the chain sees (BMW ST1505, PDF p. 27). On a longer inline-8 crank, where you drive the cams from matters even more. For real frequencies, every excitation order and the damper, use the Crank Torsion lab.

Lesson 29

Cooling Loop

An engine turns only part of its fuel energy into work. A large share goes into the coolant, and the cooling system has to carry it to the radiator. In a long inline-8, the hard part is getting the rear cylinders the same cold coolant as the front ones.

Thermostat

BMW primaryThe B58 has no conventional wax thermostat. An electrically driven heat management module uses a rotary valve to open and close coolant passages; the DME positions it from coolant temperature and a cylinder-head component-temperature sensor. The main coolant pump is belt-driven (the switchable pump in BMW's figure is not for the US). BMW ST1505 B58 Engine training manual, PDF pp. 42–44. exampleThe 88 °C opening point in this schematic is a generic wax-thermostat value used to teach the idea.

Heat-rejection calculator

Inline-8 problem: series flow vs. parallel feed

In series flow, each cylinder heats the coolant before it reaches the next one, so cylinder 8 runs hotter than cylinder 1. Hotter cylinders knock sooner and distort the bores and head differently. A distribution gallery that feeds every cylinder, with coolant crossing each cylinder from one side to the other (cross-flow), evens it out. The direction across the head is a choice: the B58 head flows from the hot exhaust side to the cooler intake side, for a more uniform head temperature (BMW ST1505, PDF p. 18). This is a real design decision your portfolio should argue for, with a thermal or CFD check.

textbookHeywood ch. 12; Hoag & Dondlinger, Vehicular Engine Design, cooling chapter (coolant distribution and cross-flow heads). BMW primaryB58 head cross-flow direction: BMW ST1505, PDF p. 18. modelBars assume equal heat per cylinder and an 85 °C inlet.

Lesson 11

Will It Fit?

An inline-8 is long. Before any 3D CAD, estimate block length and compare it to a bay you have measured yourself. Your Lesson 11 layout can be a hand sketch or a 2D drawing in FreeCAD's Sketcher; the full 3D model comes in Lesson 32 (CAD Dojo).

verifiedBMW's own training manuals give 91 mm cylinder spacing for three inline-6 engines with different bores: N52 (85 mm bore) and N54 (84 mm bore) in ST501, and the E46 M3's S54 (87 mm bore) in ST041. The bridge left between bores runs from 7 mm down to 4 mm, yet the spacing stays 91 mm. So bore + bridge alone does not explain the spacing. The crank also needs room in every spacing (see the right panel), and one shared spacing may let a factory reuse its machining lines; the manuals do not say which reason drove it. unverified91 mm is often quoted for the newer B58 too, but BMW's B58 manual ST1505 does not state it. Front and rear allowances are placeholder guesses.

What to measure on your 540i (G30)

1. Radiator/condenser back face to the firewall, at crankshaft height. 2. Front axle centerline to the firewall. 3. Where the steering rack and front crossmember sit under the oil pan. 4. Hood clearance above the cam covers. Photograph each measurement with a tape in frame; that is portfolio evidence.

The M5 body and the 8 Series are both on BMW's CLAR platform with your 540i. Treat that as a starting point only; bay shapes differ between models and I have no verified bay dimensions for any of them. You will need your own measurements or a body-in-white drawing.

Two limits on bore spacing

Spacing must be at least bore + bridge (the metal wall between two bores). With a main bearing between every pair of cylinders, as on the S54 (7 main bearings for 6 cylinders), it must also be at least the crank length per cylinder: one main journal + one rod journal + two crank webs (the plates that join them). Use whichever is larger. The CAD Dojo (Lesson 32) shows both.

Rebuild it in FreeCAD (free, open source)

  1. Spreadsheet. Open the Spreadsheet workbench and choose Spreadsheet → New Spreadsheet (Create spreadsheet in FreeCAD 1.0). Double-click Spreadsheet in the tree to open it. Click cell A1 and paste (Ctrl+V) what Copy for FreeCAD spreadsheet gave you. Then name each value cell: select B1, press Ctrl+Shift+A and type the alias bore. Repeat for B2 to B10, using the names in column A.
  2. Deck sketch. Open Part Design. Create a body, then a sketch on the XY plane. Draw a rectangle from the origin up and to the right, so its lower-left corner sits on the origin. Add a length constraint to the long side; in the value box type = to open the formula editor and enter Spreadsheet.deckLength. Do the same for the short side with Spreadsheet.deckWidth. Close the sketch.
  3. Pad the sketch 200 mm and tick Reversed, so the block grows down from the deck.
  4. Bore sketch. New sketch on the XY plane. Draw one circle. Diameter =Spreadsheet.bore. Centre from the origin: horizontal =Spreadsheet.wall + Spreadsheet.bore / 2, vertical =Spreadsheet.deckWidth / 2. When the lines turn green, the sketch is fully constrained. Pocket it with type Through all.
  5. Pattern. Select the Pocket in the tree first, then choose Linear Pattern (LinearPattern in FreeCAD 1.0). Check that the mode says Transform tool shapes; Transform body would copy the whole block. Direction Horizontal sketch axis. Set Mode to Spacing (Offset in 1.0), not the default Extent (Overall Length in 1.0), which would fit all the bores into one spacing length. Spacing =Spreadsheet.spacing (the Offset box in 1.0), occurrences =Spreadsheet.cyl.
  6. Test the design intent. In the spreadsheet change cyl from 8 to 6 and press F5 (Edit → Recompute; Refresh in FreeCAD 1.0). The block should lose two bores and get shorter, with no errors. Then set bridge to 6: the spacing stops at the crank's 90 mm, exactly as in the lab above.

FEA (stress): split the part into small elements (the mesh), fix where it is held (boundary conditions), apply loads such as peak cylinder pressure × piston area, then read stress against the material's fatigue limit.
